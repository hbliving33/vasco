FAQ

Solar electric

How does sunshine become electricity?

Solar cells turn sunlight into DC electricity. The inverter, the system's brain, converts it to AC power your home uses. It feeds your electrical panel to run lights and appliances, and your utility meter runs backward when you produce more than you're using.

Do I need south-facing exposure?

Not necessarily. Southern exposure makes a system more effective, but many homes without it still work well for solar.

Will my property taxes go up?

Probably not. In California, adding an active solar energy system to an existing property is automatically excluded from reassessment, usually with nothing to file, and there's no limit on how much solar you can add.

FAQ

Solar pool heating

What areas do you cover?

We install and service systems mainly in Orange County and Los Angeles County, including Aliso Viejo, Anaheim, Irvine, Newport Beach, Huntington Beach, Long Beach, Beverly Hills and surrounding communities.

What should I ask when comparing solar pool heating systems?

  • Have the manufacturer and contractor been in business longer than the warranty?
  • Is the company a licensed solar contractor with liability and workers' comp certificates?
  • Does the installer have experience with all types of solar heating systems?
  • Is the warranty backed by the original manufacturer, not a distributor?
  • Is the mounting system proven against hurricane-force winds?
  • Is the collector certified and tested by independent agencies?
  • Has the collector proven trouble-free over 20+ years of use?

Will my HOA allow solar?

Yes. California's Solar Rights Act makes it illegal for HOAs to prohibit solar installations.

Can one system heat both my pool and my household water?

No. California pool collectors are unglazed and designed to heat thousands of gallons to about 80°F. Hot water systems heat under 100 gallons to about 130°F, which takes very little energy by comparison.

Can the collectors heat a spa?

Yes. Set the spa circulation cycle for the end of the day and the thermostat to 104°F.

Will I need a pool cover?

Not during the warm swim season. A cover does hold heat overnight and cuts evaporative heat loss.

How much warmer will my pool be?

Following our recommended collector sizing, typically 6 to 20 degrees warmer than before under the same weather. More collector area relative to pool surface means warmer water.

Can my home accommodate a system? Must it face south?

Most homes can. Collectors usually go on the roof but can go anywhere with good sun for much of the day, and they don't have to face south. Size depends on your pool, location, orientation, shading and desired season.

How does a solar pool heater work?

Your existing pump sends water through the filter, then sensors and a motorized valve divert it through the collectors when there's heat available. The warmed water returns to the pool, and once it reaches your setpoint, water bypasses the collectors.

What does it cost to operate and maintain?

Nothing to operate, because the sun is free. Maintenance is minimal; normal filter cleaning is usually all that's needed, with a cleaning and service check recommended after five years.

Why heat with solar?

The U.S. Department of Energy identifies solar pool heating as one of the most cost-effective ways to cut energy use. Gas heaters release carbon dioxide and other emissions; solar uses no fossil fuel at all.

Owner's guide

Solar pool system operation

Before you start: drain the system for winter before November 15 and keep it off until March 1. Many systems drain automatically, so check with us if you're not sure.

Normal startup

  1. Confirm the pool pump is running and the power indicator is lit.
  2. Set the solar controller's function switch to Automatic.
  3. Set your temperature between 60°F and 104°F.
  4. When water enters the collectors, filter pressure rises about 4–7 psi and air purges for roughly 3 minutes (you'll see bubbles).

Backwashing the filter

  1. Turn off the controller and wait for the solar valve to move fully to Bypass.
  2. Turn the red-handle ball valve to Off (perpendicular to the pipe).
  3. Backwash as usual and add diatomaceous earth.
  4. Wait about 30 minutes, reopen the red-handle valve, and return the controller to Automatic.

Winter freeze protection

  1. Set the solar controller to Off.
  2. Turn off the main pump and wait at least one hour for drainage.
  3. Close the red-handle ball valve to isolate the collectors.
  4. Open the 1/2-inch ball valve and leave it open until spring startup.

Troubleshooting & tips

  • No "flow through collector" light: confirm Automatic, then try the manual flow setting; if still off, call us.
  • No bubbles at startup: check the pump, controller mode, connections and temperature setting.
  • Bubbles that won't stop: clean or backwash the filter and empty the pump basket.
  • Run the pump roughly 11 AM to 5 PM, pull from the main drain, and aim return jets downward.
FAQ

Solar hot water

How much does a solar hot water system cost?

On average $5,500 to $7,000, depending on the number of people in the home, system size and type, roof type and building code requirements.

Will solar affect my water heater?

Yes, in a good way. Your water heater runs less often, which can extend its life significantly.

What about maintenance?

Minimal. Solar water heating systems are simple, and ours need almost no maintenance.

How do solar water heaters work?

A heat-transfer fluid flows through the collectors, heats up, and transfers that heat to your household water in a storage tank. Solar can be your only hot water source or pair with a conventional backup for heavy demand. Systems are sized by bedrooms, occupants and household needs.
