Solar Electricity (PV)

Make your own power

Photovoltaic modules convert sunlight into electricity. Systems generate during daylight hours, peaking in the afternoon; clouds, rain and dirty panels reduce output. California has more homes with solar than any other state, and most systems are connected to the local utility grid.

When your system produces less than you use, such as at night, the utility or a battery fills the gap. When it produces more, the surplus flows back to the grid. It all switches automatically.

  • Substantially lower electric bills
  • Protection from future utility rate increases
  • Higher property value without higher property taxes
  • Fewer emissions and less dependence on oil
Solar Pool Heating

A warmer pool, for months longer

A comfortable pool becomes the gathering place for family and friends. A Vasco system lengthens your swim season, makes the water warmer, and can drastically reduce or eliminate pool-heating costs. We've installed thousands of systems and worked with nearly every type of solar heating system.

Solar Industries SI-2000 collectors

  • Survived a 23-year life expectancy test in the Arizona desert
  • Tens of thousands of installations worldwide since 1976
  • Unaffected by pool chemicals; can't rust, corrode or discolor your pool
  • NSF-50 listed and rated 1,100 Btu/ft² by the Florida Solar Energy Center
  • 4 ft wide in 10 or 12 ft lengths; roof or ground-rack mounted

How it works

  1. You set your desired temperature on the automatic control panel.
  2. A solar sensor checks for enough solar energy and whether the pool is below your setpoint; it works on hazy or cloudy days too.
  3. A valve sends pool water up to the collectors, pushed by your existing pool pump.
  4. The sun heats the water as it flows through the collector tubes, then it returns to the pool, repeating until your target temperature is reached.
Solar Hot Water

Most of your home's hot water, from the sun

Heating water is one of the oldest uses of solar energy, and millions of homes and businesses do it today. A single conventional water heater can generate one to two tons of carbon dioxide per year; solar displaces that, along with sulfur dioxide, carbon monoxide and nitrous oxides.

Roof collectors absorb the sun's heat, a pump circulates water to a storage tank in your garage or utility room, and a differential temperature controller captures the maximum available energy. Every system is custom designed.

Typical two-panel system: $5,500 to $7,000, including two collectors, a solar storage tank, insulation, pump and automatic control. Payback depends on your water use and utility rates.

Title 24 Compliance

Stay compliant with California's Energy Code

Title 24, Part 6 requires solar PV on almost all new residential buildings, and the requirement reaches new builds, major renovations, ADUs and additions.

Pool heating, effective January 1, 2026: for new pools or first-time heater installations, gas can no longer be the only primary heat source. Options include solar thermal pool heating, heat pump pool heaters, or hybrid renewable systems.

We'll help your project meet the code.
